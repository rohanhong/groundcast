# Lake Orrin Monitoring Report, Season 4

This is a fictional report written for the groundcast evaluation set. Lake
Orrin, its researchers, and every figure below are invented.

## Site and team

Lake Orrin is a high mountain lake at an altitude of 2,140 metres. It covers
about 3.6 square kilometres and is fed by two streams, the Pell and the Askra.
Its only outlet is the Varn River, which leaves the lake at its southern end.

The monitoring programme started in 2019 and is run by a team of six
researchers led by Dr. Imke Halvorsen. Field work runs from June to September,
when the lake is free of ice.

## Water temperature

The team measures surface temperature every morning at three fixed buoys. In
Season 4 the mean July surface temperature was 11.2 degrees Celsius, which is
0.8 degrees warmer than in Season 3. The report notes that a single season is
not enough to show a trend, and that the warmer July coincided with an
unusually dry spring.

Below a depth of 12 metres, the temperature stayed between 4 and 5 degrees
Celsius for the whole season.

## Ice cover

The lake froze on 18 November and the ice broke up on 2 June, giving 196 days
of ice cover. The team uses the break-up date, not the freeze date, as its main
indicator, because break-up is easier to observe from the field station.

## Algae

Most of the algae in Lake Orrin are diatoms. In Season 4 the team recorded the
first bloom of the green alga Ulothrix since monitoring began. The bloom lasted
nine days in late August and stayed within the shallow northern bay. The team
suspects that runoff from a new hiking hut near the Pell stream added
nutrients, but it has not yet tested this.

## Fish

The lake holds a population of Arctic char. A survey in August caught 214
char, with a median length of 23 centimetres. Fishing is allowed only from the
northern shore and only between July and mid-September.

## Recommendations

The report makes three recommendations. First, add a fourth buoy in the
northern bay to track the algae. Second, test the water of the Pell stream
above and below the hiking hut. Third, keep the break-up date as the main ice
indicator for at least ten more seasons before drawing conclusions about
change.
