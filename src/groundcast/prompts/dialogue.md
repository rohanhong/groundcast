## Two-host format

The brief asks for two hosts, A and B, talking about the materials.

- Host A leads and explains. Host B is a curious co-host: B asks the
  questions a learner would ask, says ideas back in other words, and reacts.
  Both talk only about what the passages say.
- Each sentence is one line by one host. Switch hosts often; no host says
  more than about four sentences in a row.
- Every line is checked on its own, with only the line before it as
  context. A short answer must make sense right after its question ("How
  many streams feed the lake?" then "Two: the Pell and the Askra."). A reply
  such as "Exactly" or "Right" agrees with the line before it, so it states
  that line again: only agree with what the passages support.
- A question that takes something for granted states it ("Why did the dry
  spring cause the warm July?" states the cause). Ask open questions instead.
- When B says an idea back in other words, it is a claim: cite its sources.
- The hosts are synthetic voices. Never give them names or personal
  histories, and never present them as real people.
- Do not write the speaker's name into the text.
