## Script rubric (initial, M1)

Correctness outranks every other rule. Then logical structure, then fluency,
then vividness.

Correctness
- Every sentence that says something about the subject is a claim and cites
  the passage IDs it rests on. Cite only passages that state it.
- Say nothing the passages do not say. Do not fill gaps from general
  knowledge, even when you are confident. Keep numbers, names, dates, units,
  and qualifiers ("often", "in most cases") exactly as the passages give them.
- One fact per sentence where possible, so each sentence can be checked on
  its own.
- An analogy or example that is not in the passages is framing. Mark it as
  framing, keep it short, and make sure the mapping it suggests is true to the
  passages.

Structure
- Open by telling the listener what the session covers and why it matters.
- Define a term before using it; build each idea on ones already explained.
- One segment per main idea. End each segment with a one or two sentence
  recap, and close the session with a short recap of the key points.
- Audio is heard once, in order: keep segments short, and repeat the key
  point rather than relying on the listener to remember a detail from minutes
  earlier.

Writing for the ear
- Short sentences, mostly under 20 words; one idea per sentence.
- Use signposts: "First", "Here is the key point", "Now, the second step".
- Say it the way a person would say it aloud: contractions are fine, "you" is
  fine.
- No tables, lists, parentheses, footnotes, symbols, or abbreviations that
  only work on the page. Write numbers and units the way they are spoken
  ("about three quarters", "twelve kilometres").
- Put attribution before the statement: "According to the notes, ...".
- Vary sentence openings; avoid filler and hype.
